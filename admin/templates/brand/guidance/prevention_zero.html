Every general row needs a prevention row at zero

Upstream impact is the impact of having grown, made and moved the food. It happens before anything is thrown away, so it does not depend on where the waste eventually goes. Food that was never wasted, though, was never produced, and none of that impact belongs to it. That is what the destination ticked prevention on the destinations screen means: not somewhere the food went, but waste that did not happen. Its upstream row is zero.

Miss one and nothing looks wrong. The calculator goes on charging prevented food for production that never took place, and reports a smaller benefit for preventing waste than for composting it — the opposite of the point this calculator exists to make. On the numbers loaded today, the part left out is about four-fifths of the benefit.

You will meet this as a refusal rather than as a rule to remember. Publishing a factor set is refused while any combination of sector, food category and metric has a general row and no prevention row at zero to go with it. The message lists the combinations, in that order, and names the prevention destinations it looked for. Add one upstream row for each — Destination: one of those, Value per kg 0 — and publish again.

A prevention row that exists but is not zero counts as missing. The check reads the value, not the row. Publishing is refused outright for any upstream or downstream row that prices a prevention destination at something other than zero, whether or not a general row exists to compare it against.

The usual way to arrive here is adding something to a draft: a new sector, a new food category or a new metric multiplies out into general rows, and every one of them needs its own zero.

Rolling back to an archived set does not apply this check. A set archived before the rule existed will fail it, and refusing an emergency rollback over an incomplete table would be worse than the mistake the rule prevents.
