Four things, in this order

The first two are about getting into this panel safely, and nothing else here is safe to do before they are done — which is why the panel made you start both of them before it would show you this page.

  1. Change the password you were issued. Done already: no other screen opens until that password has been replaced, so if you are reading this, yours has been.

    It is forced because that password was never yours alone. It is printed in the log of the installation that created your account, and it stays there. If somebody else went through these steps for you and handed the account over working, then the password you are using now is one they chose — change it on Your security.

  2. Enrol an authenticator, and keep a way back in. The first authenticator is done already too — nobody gets in without one. Two things the panel did not make you do:

    Keep the five recovery codes. They were shown once, on the page straight after the QR code, and nothing can show them again. This system sends no email and has no “forgot password” link, so those codes are the only way back in that does not need somebody else. If you did not save them, an administrator other than you can reset your authenticator and you enrol again from the beginning — this panel refuses to let an account reset its own, so it does have to be somebody else.

    Set up a second device now, before you need it. Your security → Add an authenticator. Have your password to hand: that screen asks for it again on purpose, because a signed-in browser is not proof of who is holding it. It takes a minute, both devices go on working, and it is the difference between a lost phone being an inconvenience and being a phone call to whoever else has an account.

  3. Find the numbers the calculator is using. Factor sets. At most one row is ever marked Published, and that is the one every visitor's calculation uses; the rest are drafts or archived.

    Look along that row to is_mock — the list screens label their columns with the database's own names, so it reads like that here and Is Mock on the form. It carries a green tick today. The factors in this system are placeholders, the right shape and the wrong values, and while that tick is there every result the public sees and every file they export carries a warning saying so, which they cannot dismiss.

    That screen explains the rest, and it is worth reading before you change anything on it: what draft, published and archived mean, why a published set's numbers are never edited in place, and what has to happen when the real factors arrive.

  4. Run one calculation, to see the system answer. Try a scenario. Leave everything as it is, except Destination: choose anything other than Prevented — waste avoided, which that page explains it cannot use. Then press Run.

    Numbers coming back mean the whole chain works: this panel reached the calculation service, which read the published factor set out of the database and worked through its formulas.

    If it is refused instead, which refusal it is matters. The calculation service is not reachable means the calculator is down and this panel is not. A valid staff session is required means the calculator is running but has not been set up to accept a sign-in from this panel; that is a setting where the system was installed, not anything this screen can put right, and it is worth telling whoever installed it. Either way you can still watch the calculator answer: open the public front page, outside this panel, and put a quantity in. The placeholder warning from step 3 is on that result, which is what a visitor sees today.

Then look around

Every box on every form has a line under it saying what it is for, and the screens where the order of things matters carry a block like this one at the top. Between them they are the whole of what this panel writes down — so before you go looking for somebody to ask, read the top of the page and the line under the box.
