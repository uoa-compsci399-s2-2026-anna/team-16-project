Writing a formula

{{ _("Where each value in a formula comes from, and what makes Save refuse it") }}

A formula is worked out once for every line a visitor enters — one food category going to one destination — with four kinds of value available to it:

  • qty_kg — what the visitor entered for that line, in kilograms.
  • upstream — from the Upstream factors screen, for this line's sector, food category and metric. A row naming the line's destination wins; otherwise the row with a blank destination applies; if there is neither, it is zero.
  • downstream — from the Downstream factors screen, for this line's destination, food category and metric, with the same fallback to the row with a blank food category and the same zero if nothing matches. It may be negative: animal feed displaces feed that would otherwise have been grown, so that route is a genuine credit.
  • const_… — any row on the Constants screen belonging to this same factor set, written as const_ followed by its code. Constants belong to a factor set, so a formula can be valid in one version and not in another.

Write const_GWP_CH4, never const_GWP_CH4_100. Methane traps far more heat in its first twenty years than its hundred-year average suggests, so the greenhouse-gas answer depends on the period you count over — and the visitor chooses which. const_GWP_CH4 is whichever of the two they picked. A formula that names a horizon returns the same figure for both choices, and nothing reports it: the visitor's choice simply stops doing anything.

Write one line's contribution and stop. The calculator adds the lines up itself, and a formula can never see any line but its own. The usual expression is qty_kg * (upstream + downstream).

A formula this panel accepts is one the calculator can run. Two separate pieces of code read these expressions: this panel checks the text when you press Save, and the calculator works it out on every visitor's request. They were written independently, and a test puts the same formulas through both and fails if they disagree — the first time it ran, it found eight disagreements. That is why saving is a real check and not a courtesy. A formula that gets past this screen will not break in front of a visitor.

Saving is refused, naming the line and column, for:

  • arithmetic beyond + - * /, brackets and a leading minus. Powers (**), % and // are all refused;
  • a name the calculator does not have — a mistyped upstrem, or a constant that is not in this factor set;
  • any function but min, max, abs and round. round(qty_kg, 2) is accepted; round(qty_kg, ndigits=2) is refused, because it was once accepted and quietly ignored the 2, returning a believable number from a formula nobody had written;
  • quoted text, true and false, and anything that reaches inside a value with a dot or a square bracket. Decimal points in numbers are of course fine.
